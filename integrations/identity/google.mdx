Google Workspace signs people in to the console with **SAML 2.0**, through a custom SAML app in the Google Admin
console. The SAML app can send group names, which map to roles. To discover agents in Google Cloud, see
[Google Cloud](/integrations/gcp).

Changing single sign-on needs the **Owner** role in OpenController, and a Google Workspace super administrator. SAML
also needs a secrets manager: OpenController's signing key is kept there. Below, `<console address>` is the address
under **Settings** > **Authentication** > **Console address**.

## Connect Google Workspace

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add a custom SAML app in the Admin console">
    In the Google Admin console, go to **Apps** > **Web and mobile apps** > **Add app** > **Add custom SAML app**. Note
    the IdP ID from the SSO URL, `https://accounts.google.com/o/saml2/idp?idpid=<id>`, then set:

    - **ACS URL:** `<console address>/saml/acs`
    - **Entity ID:** `<console address>/saml/metadata`
    - **Name ID:** **Primary email**.
    - **Attribute mapping:** **Primary email** to `email`, and, for roles from groups, **Group membership** to
      `groups`, with the groups you will map.

    Turn the app on for the organizational units or groups that may use OpenController.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect Google Workspace in OpenController">
    Go to **Settings** > **Single sign-on**. Under **Identity provider**, select **Google Workspace**; under **Signs in
    with**, **SAML 2.0**. Fill in **SAML app's IdP id** and **Email domains** (every Workspace domain your people sign
    in with; anyone from another domain is refused), then select **Connect Google Workspace**.

    Check that the values under **1. In your identity provider, create a SAML application with these values** match
    the app.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The **Single sign-on** page shows **IdP Connected**. Open the console in a private window and sign in with a Workspace
account from one of your domains.

<Warning>
**Signs in with** also offers **OpenID Connect** for Google, but it does not work yet: the console sends the access
token to OpenController, which checks it as a signed JWT, and Google's access tokens are not JWTs. Google also requires a
client secret for a web OAuth client, which OpenController does not hold. Use SAML.
</Warning>

## Give people roles

A signed-in person's role comes only from **Settings** > **Role mappings**; it cannot be set per person under
**Members**.

- Under **Granted to these groups or roles**, enter the group names the `groups` attribute sends. A person in several
  mapped groups gets the highest role.
- Set **Anyone signed in whom nothing maps gets** to a default role (not **Owner**), or **no access** to refuse
  everyone else.

Changing role mappings needs **Owner**.

<Note>
Google Workspace provisions over SCIM only to apps in its own catalogue, not to a custom SAML app. Do not generate a
SCIM token under **3. Provisioning (SCIM 2.0, optional)**: once one exists, only provisioned people can sign in, and
Google would provision no one.
</Note>

## What it uses

| What | Value |
|---|---|
| SAML metadata | `https://accounts.google.com/o/saml2/idp?idpid=<id>` |
| SAML attributes read | Email from `email`, `mail` or a Name ID with `@`; groups from `groups`; roles from `roles` or `role` |
| Stored | OpenController's own SAML signing key, in your secrets manager. Nothing of Google's. |

Signing out ends only the console session; the Google session stays.
