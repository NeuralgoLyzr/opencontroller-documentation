## Secrets

OpenController keeps every secret in your organization's secrets manager. Values are **write-only**: you set or rotate them here, but nobody can read them back. Only the server reads them. They are never shown, logged or returned by the API.

### Where secrets are kept

**Kept in** names the store, chosen when OpenController is deployed (`SECRETS_PROVIDER`): **HashiCorp Vault** (KV version 2), **AWS Secrets Manager**, **Azure Key Vault** or **Google Secret Manager**. **MongoDB, sealed with DISCOVERY_SECRET_KEY** seals them in OpenController's database, for development and single-node installs only. With **No secrets manager**, nothing can be stored and integrations that need a client secret cannot be added; whoever runs OpenController must set a store and restart.

### What you change here

Only **Platform** secrets (keys OpenController itself uses, for example `platform/anthropic-api-key`, the assistant's key) and **Custom** secrets (`custom/<name>`) are changed here. Integration client secrets are changed on the integration, the SAML signing key by reconnecting single sign-on, and module credentials in [Modules](/administer/modules).

- **New secret**: the **Name** takes letters, digits, `-` and `_`, and `/` to nest, for example `evals/openai-api-key`.
- **Rotate value** stores a new version (`v2`, `v3`, …). Whatever uses the secret reads the new value. The old value is not kept anywhere you can read it.
- **Delete** removes every version from the secrets manager. Whatever uses it stops working until it is set again. This cannot be undone.

### Activity

**Activity**, under the list, shows every change to a secret and who made it. Values are never recorded. It shows the latest 50 changes.

## Authentication

**Authentication** shows how people and machines prove who they are. Under **Ways in**, an **Owner** turns off the ways of signing in your organization does not use. The rest of the section is read only. You connect single sign-on under [Single sign-on](/administer/sso); the rest is set in the server's configuration.

### Ways in: turn sign-in methods off

Once everyone signs in through single sign-on, you can close the other ways into the console. **Ways in** has two switches:

| Switch | What turning it off does |
|---|---|
| **Email and password** | Nobody can sign in with an email and password. Every password session ends at once, yours too if you have one. Everyone signs in with single sign-on. |
| **Service-account key at the sign-in page** | The sign-in page stops offering the **API credential** box, and a browser signed in with a `cpk_…` key is signed out. Agents and scripts keep calling the API with their keys. |

<Steps>
  <Step title="Sign in with single sign-on">
    Sign in to the console through your identity provider, as an **Owner**. To turn off **Email and password**, single sign-on must be connected and you must have signed in with it, so it is proven to work for you first.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Turn the switch off">
    Go to **Settings** > **Authentication**. Under **Ways in**, switch off **Email and password** or **Service-account key at the sign-in page**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    Type `turn off` in the dialog, and select **Turn off**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

- Turning a way back on is never blocked. The dialog asks once, with no word to type.
- Each change is recorded in the [audit log](/administer/audit-log) as `sign_in_methods.changed`. Under the switches, **Last changed by** says who made the last change, and when.
- When a switch is greyed out, the reason is under its name: only an **Owner** can change it, single sign-on is not connected yet, or you did not sign in with single sign-on.
- Single sign-on has no switch here. It is turned off by disconnecting it under [Single sign-on](/administer/sso#change-or-disconnect).

<Info>Whoever runs OpenController can fix either way in the server's configuration: `DISCOVERY_PASSWORD_LOGIN` and `DISCOVERY_CREDENTIAL_LOGIN`, `true` or `false`. A value set there wins. The switch then shows *Set in the server's configuration* and cannot be changed here.</Info>

<Warning>If single sign-on stops working while **Email and password** is off, nobody can sign in with a password. Whoever runs OpenController sets `DISCOVERY_PASSWORD_LOGIN=true` and restarts it to get back in.</Warning>

### Sign-in methods and sessions

**Sign-in methods** lists each way of proving who you are, on or off: **Single sign-on (OpenID Connect)**, **Single sign-on (SAML)**, **SCIM provisioning**, **Service-account keys** and **Static API keys**.

**Static API keys** are kept in the secret `platform/api-keys`. Prefer `cpk_…` keys from [Service accounts](/administer/service-accounts).

Sessions default to an **Idle timeout** of 12 hours and a **Longest session** of 168 hours (7 days). **Console address** is where browsers reach the console, and single sign-on's addresses (redirect URI, SAML Reply URL) start with it. It shows "not set" when the deployment has not set `DISCOVERY_PUBLIC_URL`.

## Who can use it

- **Secrets** and **Authentication** need the **Admin** role, to read them as well as to change secrets.
- Only an **Owner** can turn a way in off or on under **Ways in** (the `sso:write` permission).
- Only an **Owner** can change the secrets that decide who signs in: `platform/api-keys`, `platform/handoff-key` and `platform/scim-token` (the `secrets:sign-in` permission).
