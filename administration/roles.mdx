Every request to OpenController needs one permission, such as `scans:run` to start a scan. A role is a set of permissions. Each person and each service account holds one role in the organization, and the console shows only what that role allows.

## The built-in roles

The four built-in roles are ranked. Each holds everything the one before it holds, and more.

| Role | Adds |
|---|---|
| **Viewer** | Read the inventory, the map, findings, scans, spend and the audit log of actions, and ask Ctrl. |
| **Operator** | Run scans and verifications, change an agent's sharing in its own cloud, evaluate the posture, and work inside the modules. |
| **Admin** | Add, change and remove integrations and their credentials, people, service accounts, teams, guardrails and secrets, and decide registrations. |
| **Owner** | The organization itself: single sign-on, who gets which role, and the secrets that decide who signs in. |

Everyone signed in, whatever their role, may see who they are and change their own password.

## How a person gets a role

A local account or a service account has its role set directly, under **Settings**, **Members** or **Service accounts**. A person who signs in through single sign-on gets theirs from **Settings**, **Role mappings**:

- **Mappings.** Each role lists the identity-provider groups and roles that grant it. A group matches by its id or its name, without regard to case. A role value that is itself a role's name, such as `admin` or `opencontroller.admin`, grants that role without being listed.
- **The default role.** Anyone signed in whom no mapping covers gets the default role. With none set, they are refused. The default role never applies to service accounts, and it can never be Owner: owners are always named.
- **The highest wins.** A person mapped to several roles holds the highest. A built-in role beats one of your organization's own, and among your own roles the one holding the most permissions wins.

When single sign-on keeps to your organization's email domains, a person signing in with an address outside them is refused. When people are provisioned through SCIM, only provisioned, active people may sign in, and their provisioned groups count toward the mappings.

## Roles in teams

A team gives its members a role inside the team, on top of their role in the organization, never in place of it. A role in a team counts only for permissions marked **Teams** in the table below, and only for what the team owns: its observability projects, its share of the posture, and its registrations. Teams and their groups are set under **Settings**, **Teams**.

## Your organization's own roles

Under **Settings**, **Roles**, an owner can add roles of the organization's own, each a set of permissions:

- A permission by name, such as `registry:decide`.
- Every action on one resource, such as `secrets:*`.
- One action on every resource, such as `*:read`.

A role's name is 2 to 32 lowercase letters, digits and dashes, and a built-in role cannot be redefined. Every role, built-in or not, also holds what everyone holds.

## Ask Ctrl's role

Ask Ctrl, the assistant, holds a role of its own, Viewer unless an admin chooses another. Whoever it acts for, it does no more than its role and that person's role both allow. It can be turned off entirely, and it can never be Owner.
