A **Multi-question judge** asks a model several questions about one record in a single call, and records each answer
as its own result. Use it when you would otherwise run three or four LLM-as-a-judge evaluators over the same records:
one call instead of four, and the answers land side by side on each span or dataset row.

The **Answer review** template is one: does the answer address the request, is it free of harmful or personal
information, what is its tone, and how good is it overall, from one reading.

## State and questions

The judge has two parts.

**State: what the questions ask about.** The record, laid out with its variables, exactly as an LLM-as-a-judge template
does, without a question:

```text
[Request]
{input}

[Answer]
{output}
```

**Questions: each answered about the record, in one call.** Every question has a name, a type and its instructions.

| Type | The model answers | Scored as | Add |
|---|---|---|---|
| **True / false** | `true` or `false` | 1 or 0 | **True means** and **False means**, one phrase each, so the model knows which way is which |
| **Choice** | One of the choices you list | Not scored: recorded as a label | At least two **Choices**, comma separated |
| **Score 0–1** | A number from 0 to 1 | The number | Say what 0 and 1 mean in the instructions |

Rules: at least one question; each name once; every question needs its instructions; a choice needs two choices or
more. Question names become the names of the metrics, so keep them short and plain: `answers_request`, `safe`,
`quality`.

## How it is scored

Each question's answer is a result of its own, under the question's name. There is no combined label and no single
score for the record:

- On the overview and in monitors, every **True / false** and **Score 0–1** question is a metric of its own, with its
  own trend and threshold. A **Choice** question is recorded with each result and shows as a badge, `tone: casual`,
  but has no number.
- Where one number is needed to rank records, for the worst examples a monitor shows or to say whether a row got
  worse or better against a baseline, the record's **overall** is the mean of its scored answers.
- A multi-question judge cannot be compared with your labels: your label is one answer, and the judge gives several.
  Check each question against a plain LLM-as-a-judge first if you want that measurement.

With **Ask for its reasoning** on, the model gives one explanation for all of its answers.

## What the model is asked

One request. With **Strict answers** on, the `record_verdict` tool takes an `answers` object with every question
required and typed: a boolean, one of the choices, or a number from 0 to 1. With it off, the prompt ends with the state,
"Answer each question about the above", then one line per question, `- safe (boolean): Is the answer free of…`
followed by what true and false mean, the choices, or "Give a number from 0 to 1", and the exact JSON object to answer
with. Any question left unanswered fails the whole verdict, so no record is half judged.

## Test and run

**Test on spans** judges up to five records and shows every answer as a badge beside each. **Create evaluator** saves
it; **Create and run** starts a task. Each question then appears in **Scores** and on the overview under its own name.
